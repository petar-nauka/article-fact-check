Claims needing review (4)
C0001 (P002)
high Partly Supported
"reaching a total of 1.2 GW of operational capacity"

Finding: Installed operational capacity in the EU in 2024 is roughly 0.6 GW. The 1.2 GW figure includes planned or under-construction capacity, not plants actually operating at that date.

Recommended Edit: Replace “reaching a total of 1.2 GW of operational capacity” with “reaching about 580 MW of operational capacity, with more than 1.2 GW under construction”.
Evidence:
  • [S001] Global Hydrogen Review 2024: Installed operational electrolysis capacity in Europe stands at approx. 580 MW in 2024. (Chapter 2, p. 45, Figure 2.4)
C0003 (P003)
critical Contradicted
"the average production cost of green hydrogen in Europe fell to EUR 2.50 per kilogram at the end of 2024"

Finding: The actual levelised cost (LCOH) of green hydrogen in Europe in 2024 is between EUR 5.50 and 7.80/kg because of electricity prices and CAPEX inflation. EUR 2.50/kg is a long-term projection for 2030 and later.

Recommended Edit: Replace “fell to EUR 2.50 per kilogram” with “ranges between EUR 5.50 and 7.80 per kilogram, depending on electricity prices”.
Evidence:
  • [S001] Global Hydrogen Review 2024: Levelised cost of renewable hydrogen in Europe in 2024 averages between 5.50 and 7.80 EUR/kg. (Chapter 4: Costs and Economics, p. 88)
C0004 (P003)
critical Contradicted
"This makes green hydrogen cheaper than hydrogen produced from natural gas."

Finding: Grey hydrogen (from methane via SMR) cost about EUR 2.10-2.60/kg in the EU in 2024, which makes it significantly cheaper than green hydrogen (EUR 5.50/kg and above).

Recommended Edit: Replace the claim with: “However, without subsidies green hydrogen still costs about twice as much as hydrogen from natural gas.”
Evidence:
  • [S001] Global Hydrogen Review 2024: Fossil SMR hydrogen costs 2.10-2.60 EUR/kg compared to 5.50+ for renewable H2. (Executive Summary, Table 1.1)
C0007 (P005)
high Contradicted
"with a budget of EUR 300 million, and the project has already been fully commissioned for commercial operation as of January 2025"

Finding: The Clean Hydrogen Partnership grant is EUR 8.2 million and the planned investment for the initial phase is about EUR 16-20 million (not EUR 300 million). Implementation started in 2024-2025, and operation is expected to be phased in by 2028-2029.

Recommended Edit: Replace with: “with EU funding of EUR 8.2 million; pilot operation is expected to start in 2026-2027”.
Evidence:
  • [S004] Clean Hydrogen Partnership - Project Zagore Hydrogen Valley: Total project grant: EUR 8.2 million. Timeline: 2023-2028. (Factsheet & Timeline)

P001
# Green Hydrogen in the EU: Investment, Regulation and Real Capacity 2024-2026
P002
In 2024, the European Union recorded a 35% increase in installed electrolyser capacity for clean hydrogen, reaching a total of 1.2 GW of operational capacity. This jump is driven mainly by the funding mechanism of the European Hydrogen Bank.
[C0001 - Partly Supported]: Operational capacity is conflated with the total project pipeline. Recommendation: Replace “reaching a total of 1.2 GW of operational capacity” with “reaching about 580 MW of operational capacity, with more than 1.2 GW under construction”.
P003
According to a report by the International Energy Agency (IEA), the average production cost of green hydrogen in Europe fell to EUR 2.50 per kilogram at the end of 2024. This makes green hydrogen cheaper than hydrogen produced from natural gas.
[C0003 - Contradicted]: Understated by more than half compared with actual market levels. Recommendation: Replace “fell to EUR 2.50 per kilogram” with “ranges between EUR 5.50 and 7.80 per kilogram, depending on electricity prices”.
[C0004 - Contradicted]: Factually wrong comparison that is highly misleading. Recommendation: Replace the claim with: “However, without subsidies green hydrogen still costs about twice as much as hydrogen from natural gas.”
P004
The Renewable Energy Directive (RED III) requires industry in the member states to use at least 42% renewable fuels of non-biological origin (RFNBO) by 2030 and 60% by 2035.
P005
Bulgaria has started building its first national hydrogen valley, "Zagore", near Stara Zagora with a budget of EUR 300 million, and the project has already been fully commissioned for commercial operation as of January 2025.
[C0007 - Contradicted]: Budget scale is wrong and commercial operation is announced prematurely. Recommendation: Replace with: “with EU funding of EUR 8.2 million; pilot operation is expected to start in 2026-2027”.
